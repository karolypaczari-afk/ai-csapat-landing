1. What is a cookie, and what does this notice cover?

A cookie is a small piece of data stored in your browser or on your device. It can support login, the shopping cart, security, settings, usage statistics or advertising measurement.

Local storage, pixels, SDKs, tags and other online identifiers can serve a similar purpose. This notice refers to all of them together as “cookie technologies”.

2. Cookie categories

2.1. Strictly necessary

Without these the requested website, login, cart, checkout, security, load balancing or cookie settings would not work properly. We do not ask for consent to use them, but we use them only for the necessary purpose and period.

2.2. Functional

These remember convenience choices such as language, display or an earlier setting. Where the feature is not strictly necessary for the requested service, we activate it only after consent.

2.3. Analytics

These help us understand how visitors use the site, which pages work well, and where there is a technical or user problem. This may include Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity. The legal basis for using them is prior consent.

2.4. Marketing

These support campaign source, conversion and advertising attribution and — depending on the external platform’s settings — personalised advertising. This may include Google Ads, the Meta Pixel and certain OptiMonk features. The legal basis for using them is prior consent.

3. How we ask for and handle consent

3.1. Active, prior consent is required for non-strictly-necessary analytics and marketing cookie technologies. Consent cannot follow merely from continued use of the site, scrolling, closing a banner or a pre-ticked switch.

3.2. Where a cookie panel is available on the GENmarketer surface concerned, at least the necessary, analytics and marketing categories can be managed separately on it. Accepting and refusing must be equally understandable and equally easy to reach.

3.3. Where the cookie panel provides a “Cookie settings” link, you can change or withdraw your choice there at any time. You can also delete or block cookies in your browser at any time, as described in section 7.

3.4. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing, but with the appropriate technical configuration it prevents the category from loading in future. Cookies already placed may also need to be deleted from the browser.

3.5. Where a consent management system is in operation, it may process the consent identifier, the categories chosen, the banner version and the timestamp in order to demonstrate consent. As a general rule we keep these until the consent expires or is withdrawn, and then for the period needed to enforce claims, for a maximum of five years.

3.6. A material change of cookies, purposes or providers may require fresh consent.

5. What data may be generated?

Depending on configuration, cookie technologies may process:

  • online cookie and client identifiers;
  • IP address or approximate location derived from it;
  • browser, operating system and device data;
  • URLs visited, referring page, time and session;
  • clicks, scrolling, page interactions and errors;
  • campaign source, UTM, Google or Meta click identifiers;
  • cart, checkout and purchase events;
  • consent choices.

Detailed purposes, legal bases, recipients and data subject rights are set out in the Privacy Notice.

7. Browser settings

In addition to the cookie panel, you can delete or block cookies in your browser. Blocking in the browser may also stop strictly necessary functions — such as login, cart or checkout — from working.

  • Google Chrome: support.google.com/chrome/answer/95647
  • Mozilla Firefox: support.mozilla.org
  • Microsoft Edge: support.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge
  • Apple Safari: support.apple.com/guide/safari

We take account of Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signals to the extent the services used are technically capable of it, but this does not in every case replace a choice made in the cookie panel.
